# `draw_frame` replay bar call and coordinates

The retained `draw_frame` candidate used `rectfill` for the replay scroller bar at historical `main.c:2808`. The original instructions at offsets 4801–4895 call through `GFX_VTABLE +0xbc`. Allegro 4.4.1 `include/allegro/gfx.h` maps `+0xbc` to `rect`; `rectfill` is `+0x3c`. The historical line table also attributes the call to `draw.inl:112`, the inline `rect` definition. This is a wrong renderer call in the candidate, even though both routines accept the same argument shape.

The same original block adds `0x13` and `0x14` to stack `cx` (`-0x178(%ebp)`) for its two y coordinates. The retained candidate added `0x1d` and `0x1e`, shifting the outline by 10. The candidate now calls `rect` with `cx + 0x14` and `cx + 0x13`. `takeover-draw-bar-coordinates-20260923` separately records the coordinate correction; `takeover-draw-bar-rect-20260923` records the call correction. The latter compiled candidate calls through `+0xbc` and is 8,510/8,518 bytes. It remains `DIFFER`, with no production status change. The combined historical-order unit keeps isolated `new_game` strictly exact and `run_demo` different.

An independent vtable-call inventory found one more supported draw path missing from that retained source: the original has four calls through `+0x50` (`draw_sprite_h_flip`), while the candidate had three. Original offset 8237 is the `sx != 0` arm of the overlay draw at line 2638. The candidate's comments acknowledged this arm but omitted it because adding it increased compiled size. That size-driven omission is not valid reconstruction. A first reading treated edge==0 and edge==1 as entering one shared overlay block at offset 3115; the candidate had duplicated its C source to stay close in byte count. `takeover-draw-shared-flip-20260923` restored the flip arm and one source overlay block. The resulting candidate was 8,175/8,518 and remained `DIFFER`, but its virtual-call count equaled the original for every observed slot: `+0x44` 18, `+0x48` 18, `+0x50` 4, `+0xa4` 2, and `+0xbc` 1. Later predecessor tracing corrected the edge==1 interpretation below. Call-count equality is only a structural diagnostic; instruction, relocation, and whole-TU equality are still unproved.

The original has direct-call multiplicity equal to this candidate (64 calls). The main remaining annotated deficits are lines 2606 and 2638, at 95 and 75 bytes respectively, but those counts include compiler block duplication. Original offsets 1952, 3406, and 6066 write `1` to `%esi` within the DWARF `p_im` location ranges. The current candidate does not reliably account for their conditions. The absent `custom.frame[0]` null guard remains unsupported; the p_im writes are a separate unresolved fact. Trace predecessors and variable lifetimes before adding any assignment.

The predecessor trace locates three original `p_im=1` writes. Original offset 1917 resets `ply->frame` on the `+0.2` comparison route and writes `p_im=1` at 1952; offset 3384 does likewise on the `-0.2` route and writes at 3406. An initial interpretation coupled offset 6066 solely to the overflow reset at 6032. `takeover-draw-p-im-reset-20260923` and `takeover-draw-p-im-separate-speed-20260923` record that trial. A deeper trace showed that `jle` at 6030 skips the reset but lands at 6051 and still writes `p_im=1` at 6066. The trial's placement was wrong and is reverted in the retained source. Original status-zero path at 2256–2329 can also reset `frame` and enter the edge path without a `p_im=1` write. Do not count untyped candidate `mov $1,%esi` opcodes as `p_im` writes: later dataflow inspection found this register also holds `1 - custom.frame[0]->h`. Reconstruct these branches together before restoring assignments; the old probes are diagnostic failed experiments, not accepted recovery.

The original branch at offset 2631 tests `p_im` and jumps to the edge-sprite block at 2329 when it is zero. Calls on that edge route jump to offset 2819, after the ordinary rotate/frame sprite call. The previous candidate inverted the condition and fell through to draw an additional sprite. `takeover-draw-p-im-exclusive-edge-20260923` tested the observed exclusive paths. After reverting the unsupported reset-placement trial, `takeover-draw-exclusive-no-guess-p-im-20260923` compiles the retained candidate at 8,153/8,518 bytes, still `DIFFER`; the whole historical-order unit remains 62/82 exact with no loss. This is control-flow evidence, not a size-based promotion. The status-zero bypass and the original three separate `p_im=1` sites remain unresolved prerequisites for exact `draw_frame` matching.

The speed predicate at line 2597 was also inverted in the candidate. On the positive route, original `fldl 0.2; fucompp; sahf; jbe` at 1900–1911 skips the reset when `sx>=0.2`; on the negative route, original `fldl -0.2; fxch; fucompp; sahf; jbe` at 3365–3378 skips it when `sx<=-0.2`. Thus the reset occurs inside the open `(-0.2, 0.2)` band on those routes. `takeover-draw-frame-speed-band-20260923` corrects that predicate in the retained candidate, while leaving the unresolved status-zero routing separate. It compiles to 8,145/8,518 bytes, still `DIFFER`, with the combined 62/82 exact set unchanged. Status zero enters a separate `(-0.02, 0.02)` comparison at 2256–2308; this branch can reset `frame` and go straight to the edge-sprite block without the `p_im` test at 2631. It must be recovered before treating the current C block as complete.

`draw-frame-status-zero-trial.c` is a separate diagnostic attempt at that routing. It represents the narrow speed band with a direct jump to the edge sprite and gives the outer band a `p_im=1` route, then treats `p_im!=1` as an unconditional frame reset. `takeover-draw-status-zero-route-trial-20260923` compiles to 8,327/8,518 bytes and retains the same 62 exact functions. Its source shape is not accepted into `draw_frame-merged.c`: it references each `±0.02` double twice, while the original references each once. The trial remains a concrete CFG experiment with an object hash in the probe record; its nearer byte size is not evidence of correctness. Next recover a shared narrow-band comparison and trace the status-zero edge join before changing the retained body.

`draw-frame-status-zero-join-trial.c` tests moving that outer status-zero route past the range test to the downstream `p_im=1` block, rather than assigning 1 before the test. `takeover-draw-status-zero-join-trial-20260923` compiles to 8,326/8,518 and still duplicates both `±0.02` literal references. The apparent four `mov $1,%esi` sites in this trial feed the `1 - custom.frame[0]->h` calculation in `%esi`; they are not four identified `p_im` assignments. Both trial inputs are frozen separately and their hashes match their probe records. Stop reshaping this local `goto` join without new evidence; the next cause is the missing shared comparison topology or upstream status nesting.

The duplicated data references identified the actual predicate error. Original offsets 2256–2308 use `±0.02` only for the status-zero near-still route. The nonzero pose route at 2561–2608 and 3323–3346 uses `±0.01` directly, with strict comparisons and zero included. The retained candidate had wrongly put a `±0.02` guard and an `sx!=0` guard around its pose test. `takeover-draw-pose-inner-band-20260923` removes both and uses the strict `(-0.01,0.01)` test. It is still `DIFFER` at 8,081/8,518 because it does not yet contain the status-zero comparison. In `draw-frame-status-zero-pose-trial.c`, the separately traced status-zero route supplies one reference to each `±0.02` literal; `takeover-draw-status-zero-pose-trial-20260923` compiles to 8,254/8,518 with the original one-reference multiplicity for all six `±0.02`, `±0.01`, and `±0.2` doubles. `takeover-draw-status-zero-pose-retained-20260923` verifies the same source in the retained candidate. Original and candidate virtual-call slot counts remain identical (`+0x44`:18, `+0x48`:18, `+0x50`:4, `+0xa4`:2, `+0xbc`:1); both `new_game` and `run_demo` remain strict matches in the 62/82 combined historical-order probe. `draw_frame` itself remains `DIFFER`, and production is unchanged. The earlier trial rejection based on duplicated `±0.02` references applied to the old pose guard; it does not apply to this corrected combined source.

The original has six references to `custom.frame[0]` in `draw_frame`: five height loads at offsets 1924, 2315, 2615, 3391, and 6051, plus the indexed pose lookup at 2666. This local source spelling does not solve the layout mechanism; keep the original combined predicate. Trace where the original status and speed predecessors acquire distinct live registers before their `1 - frame[0]->h` fragments, rather than duplicating a source statement to target the load count.

The edge predecessor trace resolves the earlier mistake: original offset 2346 sends edge==0 to the overlay block at 3115. For edge==1, the sprite call at 2494 jumps directly to the post-draw continuation at 2819; for edge==2, the flipped sprite call at 3552 does the same. The retained candidate used a second `if(edge!=2)`, so edge==1 drew both its sprite and the overlay. `takeover-draw-edge-zero-exclusive-20260923` makes that block the `else` of `if(edge)`. It compiles at 8,216/8,518 bytes, remains `DIFFER`, and keeps the original virtual-call multiplicities and the combined 62/82 exact functions without losses. This is a real edge-rendering correction; byte-size movement is not its basis. Production is unchanged.

The other p_im==0 route loads the same pointer at 2615 and also reaches 2329; later frame 9/10/11 selections overwrite `%edx` only when their predicates pass. The combined probe retains 62/82 exact functions, `draw_frame` remains `DIFFER` at 8,207/8,518, and the candidate still has four physical plain frame-zero loads versus the original five. The assignment fixes asset identity on the default path; it does not claim the missing GCC block duplication is solved.
